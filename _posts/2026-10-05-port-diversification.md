Peru’s emerging Pacific position should not be understood through Chancay alone. Its deeper strategic advantage may lie precisely in the fact that no single external actor controls the country’s entire maritime architecture.

Chancay anchors COSCO and Chinese trans-Pacific logistics. Callao brings together DP World and APM Terminals within Peru’s largest metropolitan and commercial system. Pisco introduces the MSC–TiL network, while Matarani connects southern Peru to another configuration of Peruvian and global infrastructure capital. Paita extends this plurality toward the northern coast.

What appears as fragmentation can become **strategic diversification**.

The objective is not to exclude foreign capital. Peru lacks both the necessity and the advantage of choosing between Chinese, North American, European or Gulf networks. The more interesting strategy is to ensure that extraction, finance, ports, shipping, processing, energy and market access are never vertically captured by a single external system.

This transforms foreign investment from simple dependency into a field of competing relations. COSCO needs Peruvian territory and cargo; MSC needs competitive terminals and hinterlands; DP World and APM need expanding trade; global infrastructure funds seek durable flows. Each relationship can increase Peru’s *potentia* provided that none becomes capable of organizing the whole.

The state’s strategic function consequently changes. It need not operate every port. It must **organize the architecture within which operators compete, interconnect and remain replaceable**.

This is particularly important as Peru reaches beyond its own territory. Matarani and Ilo face Bolivia and the Brazilian-Paraguayan-Argentine interior; Pisco opens another transversal axis; Callao and Chancay provide metropolitan mass and trans-Pacific scale. Even Antofagasta could become a multimodal interface through which continental cargo enters a wider Pacific network.

A Peruvian *nomos* would therefore not mean territorial expansion or geopolitical alignment. It would mean constructing a spatial order in which multiple global powers depend upon connections organized through Peru.

**Sovereignty, in such a system, comes not from isolation but from diversification: no indispensable relationship should become an irreplaceable one.**
